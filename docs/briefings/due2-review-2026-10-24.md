## 1. Decision summary

**The idea is reasonable and worth continuing. The present revision is not ready to be treated as an authoritative oracle for all of its specified semantics, or as an implementation-ready foundation without further design corrections.**

The strongest parts are the explicit distinction between unknown and false, evidence-derived fulfilment, the normalization/evaluation separation, nominal duty identity, the structured explanation model, and the deliberate use of several different assurance mechanisms. The executable semantics has substantial tests, and the checks reproduced locally are genuinely green.

However, green tests do not establish that the specified interfaces are sufficient, that the laws are mutually consistent, or that every accepted policy is ODRL-conformant. Independent counterexamples expose those gaps:

1. The flat corpus contract does not specify a general lossless representation of disjunctive duty conditions.
2. W2's published output omits a deadline fact W3 needs when fulfilment is unknown.
3. A supposedly void policy can still deny through one of its obligations.
4. ODRL replacement metadata is accepted and ignored, allowing the replaced policy to permit.
5. Several static rejections asserted by the specification are not implemented by the admissibility checker.
6. The ODRL validation layer accepts invalid examples and rejects an example using mandatory inverse-target semantics.
7. Several stated laws need qualification or repair before they can serve as acceptance contracts.

These are not reasons to discard the design. They are reasons to complete the semantic contract and qualify the oracle before depending on it. In particular, **do not start by making another evaluator agree with due2 on these examples**. First decide what due2 should mean, record that decision, and pin the independent counterexample.

## 2. What due2 is trying to achieve

The intended computation is a policy decision over four frozen inputs: a normalized corpus, a request, a state-of-the-world snapshot, and configuration. The design separates three responsibilities:

- **W1:** translate authored ODRL into a bounded, flat, provenance-carrying representation, once per policy version.
- **W2:** evaluate matching, constraints, and duty performance against the frozen request and world, preserving `T`, `F`, and `U`.
- **W3:** resolve candidates and conflicts, compute duty routes, and decide whether all allowed completions agree on a verdict.

The result is richer than an access-control Boolean: `Permit`, `Deny`, `Pending`, `NotApplicable`, or `Indeterminate`, together with reasons. `Pending` preserves actionable precondition routes. `Indeterminate` records disagreement between completions rather than treating failed lookups as false. A separate monitoring question is deliberately not yet specified.

This decomposition is understandable and implementable. ODRL does not itself define due2's five-verdict calculus, completion algorithm, evidence protocol, or cross-policy deployment defaults. Those are due2's semantic choices. Correctness therefore has three different obligations:

1. The choices must be coherent and defensible as due2 semantics.
2. Supported ODRL constructs must be interpreted consistently with the Recommendation, or clearly identified as a restricted/extended interpretation.
3. The implementation, tests, and formal abstractions must actually implement the chosen semantics, with their common assumptions disclosed.

The review keeps these obligations separate. Disagreement with another evaluator is not automatically a due2 defect. Conversely, matching a hand-derived corpus is not proof that the common reading of the specification is right.

## 5. Detailed findings

### R01 — Disjunctive duties are admitted, but their flat representation is not specified

**Evidence.** The [corpus relations](https://github.com/matthiasautrata/due2/blob/dc232b5f2fb2442c92ea3d6e5d3a39d05c106c11/docs/spec/due2-workflows.md?plain=1#L88-L121) make constraints conjunctive per atom, with no constraint-tree, clause-group, or alternative-performance relation. The [builder contract](https://github.com/matthiasautrata/due2/blob/dc232b5f2fb2442c92ea3d6e5d3a39d05c106c11/docs/spec/due2-workflows.md?plain=1#L275-L296) eliminates disjunction by DNF. Nested duty expansion, however, gives a parent every resulting child conjunctively. [L9](https://github.com/matthiasautrata/due2/blob/dc232b5f2fb2442c92ea3d6e5d3a39d05c106c11/docs/spec/due2-workflows.md?plain=1#L539) separately says duty guards are not rewritten. [The architecture](https://github.com/matthiasautrata/due2/blob/dc232b5f2fb2442c92ea3d6e5d3a39d05c106c11/docs/design/due2-architecture.md?plain=1#L141-L159) says guards and witness conditions remain on the duty atom.

**Counterexample.** A permission requires one compensation duty whose witness condition is “amount is 5 **or** amount is 10.” One admissible witness with amount 5 fulfils the authored duty. The direct interpreter correctly returned `Permit` for this input; see `disjunctive-duty:witness-5` in the reproducer.

Naively applying the stated DNF-to-atoms rule produces a duty for amount 5 and a duty for amount 10. If both become children of the permission, the permission now needs both, changing OR into AND. Keeping the original OR tree instead violates the current flat schema. This particular example can be reduced to `isAnyOf`, but that does not provide a general lowering for arbitrary disjunctions over different witness operands or compound branches.

**Conclusion.** This is a **confirmed missing contract**, not a claim that an existing W1 implementation produces a wrong answer: W1 does not yet exist. The authored interpreter can handle a construct that the downstream interface does not explain how to represent losslessly. The same question affects disjunctive guards and root obligations, where reporting two separately required obligations is not equivalent to one obligation with alternative performances.

**Disposition before W1 stage 2.** Decide whether to retain a bounded expression/group representation, specify a semantics-preserving lowering into parent alternatives and duty identities, or explicitly narrow admitted duty expressions. Add hand-derived positive and negative vectors and require L9 on them. Do not silently choose a lowering while coding the builder.

### R02 — Unknown fulfilment hides whether the deadline has passed

**Evidence.** W2's [signature](https://github.com/matthiasautrata/due2/blob/dc232b5f2fb2442c92ea3d6e5d3a39d05c106c11/docs/spec/due2-workflows.md?plain=1#L297-L315) and the [trace relations](https://github.com/matthiasautrata/due2/blob/dc232b5f2fb2442c92ea3d6e5d3a39d05c106c11/docs/spec/due2-workflows.md?plain=1#L190-L224) expose fulfilment truth and instance status. When fulfilment is `U`, instance status is always `unknown`. Neither relation exposes `deadline_passed`. W3's [signature](https://github.com/matthiasautrata/due2/blob/dc232b5f2fb2442c92ea3d6e5d3a39d05c106c11/docs/spec/due2-workflows.md?plain=1#L400-L408) does not even list `instance`, although subsequent prose requires W3 to use it.

**Reproduced pair.** The corpus, request, config, and evidence are identical. The duty's deadline is 2026-06-02. A timely compensation record has no value for its required amount, leaving fulfilment unknown.

| World observation time | `applies` | `owed` | `fulfilled` | Instance status | Correct completion range returned by clingo-i |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 2026-06-01 | T | T | U | unknown | Pending → Permit |
| 2026-06-03 | T | T | U | unknown | NotApplicable → Permit |

In the later world, completing fulfilment to false closes the candidate because the deadline has passed. In the earlier world, the same completion leaves an outstanding duty. The normative W2 data described above is identical, but W3 must distinguish the worlds without reading them.

The executable program succeeds because it has an extra internal fact: [`passed(Y)`](https://github.com/matthiasautrata/due2/blob/dc232b5f2fb2442c92ea3d6e5d3a39d05c106c11/spikes/clingo-i/lp/duty.lp#L405-L420), consumed by [`violated(W,Y)`](https://github.com/matthiasautrata/due2/blob/dc232b5f2fb2442c92ea3d6e5d3a39d05c106c11/spikes/clingo-i/lp/resolve.lp#L30-L38). This is exactly the fact missing from the published interface. The same distinction matters for `violations = deny` on obligations.

**Disposition.** Carry the fixed, per-instance deadline comparison across the boundary explicitly, and state W3's complete input/output contract. Completion may then combine that fixed fact with completed fulfilment without inspecting SOTW. Add the before/after pair as a relation-level test. This is an interface repair, not permission for W3 to read the clock or world.

### R03 — “A void policy contributes nothing” is not what the code and model do

**Evidence.** [Conflict resolution](https://github.com/matthiasautrata/due2/blob/dc232b5f2fb2442c92ea3d6e5d3a39d05c106c11/docs/spec/due2-workflows.md?plain=1#L423-L427) says an `invalid` conflict eliminates all of a policy's atoms and that the void policy contributes nothing. The [architecture](https://github.com/matthiasautrata/due2/blob/dc232b5f2fb2442c92ea3d6e5d3a39d05c106c11/docs/design/due2-architecture.md?plain=1#L190-L197) likewise says the policy does not govern. The executable [obligation denial rule](https://github.com/matthiasautrata/due2/blob/dc232b5f2fb2442c92ea3d6e5d3a39d05c106c11/spikes/clingo-i/lp/resolve.lp#L100-L110) does not test whether the obligation's policy is void. The [Alloy resolution module](https://github.com/matthiasautrata/due2/blob/dc232b5f2fb2442c92ea3d6e5d3a39d05c106c11/formal/alloy/resolve.als#L94-L115) shares that reading.

**Reproduced input.** One policy, `conflict = invalid`, has an applicable permission, an applicable prohibition on the same action/target, and an overdue, unfulfilled root obligation.

- Under `violations = ignore`: `NotApplicable`, but the obligation is still reported.
- Under `violations = deny`: `Deny`, caused solely by that obligation.
- Only the permission and prohibition are listed as eliminated; the obligation remains operative.

**Conclusion.** The prose and implementation cannot both be the complete contract. Under the plain “contributes nothing” reading, the expected access verdict is `NotApplicable`, not `Deny`. If obligations are deliberately retained after invalidation, that exception needs to be stated and defended, including its ODRL interpretation.

**Important consequence.** This is not safely fixed by adding one guard without revisiting the mathematics. If voiding also removes a denying obligation, adding a prohibition under `invalid` can remove the denial and raise the verdict. That affects monotonicity claims and the optimized completion argument. Decide the semantic policy first, then re-check the proof, models, and independent oracle. The existing Alloy results cannot settle which reading is intended because the model currently adopts the implementation's reading.

### R04 — Mandatory replacement metadata is swallowed by the annotation rule

The W3C [Information Model §2.8](https://www.w3.org/TR/odrl-model/#metadata) requires a processor to consider a policy with `dc:isReplacedBy` void and retrieve/process the replacement. The normative obligation is more than descriptive metadata.

clingo-i marks [every Dublin Core Terms predicate as annotation](https://github.com/matthiasautrata/due2/blob/dc232b5f2fb2442c92ea3d6e5d3a39d05c106c11/spikes/clingo-i/lp/lift.lp#L184-L189). The admissibility checker does not reject replacement semantics. A simple permission policy with `dct:isReplacedBy ex:new` passed both checkers and returned `Permit`; the replacement was not supplied or processed.

**Disposition.** Preserve the pure-input architecture: either require replacement resolution in an explicitly enforced materialization/admission step, or reject this construct until supported. Do not dereference from the pure evaluator. A superseded policy must not silently remain authoritative because its replacement link happened to use a metadata namespace. Review blanket annotation allowlists for other normative exceptions.

### R05 — The admissibility checker is weaker than its declared rejection coverage

The reproducer obtained **no admission errors** for each of these examples:

| Input | Specified disposition | Actual interpreter disposition |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Typed `xsd:dateTime` right operand without a timezone | `OperatorTypeMismatch` | Rejected with `OperatorTypeMismatch` |
| Typed deadline without a timezone | `InvalidTiming` | Declined as `duty: deadline not comparable` |
| `eq` over a `due2:set` operand | `OperatorTypeMismatch` | Rejected with `OperatorTypeMismatch` |
| Mixed year-month/day-time duration as a right operand | `MixedDuration` | Rejected with `MixedDuration` |
| `FREQ=NOT_A_FREQUENCY;INTERVAL=-1` | `InvalidTiming` | Recurrence is generally unsupported; not qualified as valid |

These obligations are stated in the [diagnostic registry](https://github.com/matthiasautrata/due2/blob/dc232b5f2fb2442c92ea3d6e5d3a39d05c106c11/docs/spec/due2-diagnostics.md?plain=1#L55-L68) and [operator/timing specification](https://github.com/matthiasautrata/due2/blob/dc232b5f2fb2442c92ea3d6e5d3a39d05c106c11/docs/spec/due2-profiles.md?plain=1#L120-L162). The checker [tests duration shape for deadlines and merely searches recurrence text for `FREQ=`](https://github.com/matthiasautrata/due2/blob/dc232b5f2fb2442c92ea3d6e5d3a39d05c106c11/tools/check_corpus.py#L246-L284). Its [constraint checks](https://github.com/matthiasautrata/due2/blob/dc232b5f2fb2442c92ea3d6e5d3a39d05c106c11/tools/check_corpus.py#L575-L613) special-case `isA` over a set, but do not implement the general single-value-operator restriction. Policy time operands are not validated by the scenario-timestamp regex.

**Impact.** A green admission run is not evidence that these policies satisfy the declared admitted subset. The negative corpus does not currently distinguish the missing checks from correct checks. For example, rejecting an untyped date string does not test rejection of a correctly typed but timezone-less datetime.

**Disposition.** Add near-miss negative cases for the actual typed shapes, make the checker coverage table accurate, and reconcile rejection versus unsupported responses. Reuse or independently cross-check lexical validation rather than treating RDF parsing or substring presence as validation. Do not change expected verdicts to bless the current omissions.

### R06 — An unknown profile gets a warning, not the required stop

W3C [IM §3.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/odrl-model/#profile-conformance) says a processing system that does not recognize a profile identifier **must stop processing the policy**.

The [admission entry point](https://github.com/matthiasautrata/due2/blob/dc232b5f2fb2442c92ea3d6e5d3a39d05c106c11/tools/check_corpus.py#L987-L1004) warns that an unknown profile's terms are metadata and disables the normal vocabulary-definition restriction for that case. A policy selecting only `ex:unknown`, otherwise containing a straightforward core permission, receives no admission error. The interpreter, correctly, returns an unknown-profile error for the same input.

**Disposition.** Separate “this graph may be structurally valid ODRL” from “this processor understands it and may evaluate it.” Unknown-profile admission must not be a successful due2 admission. A profile's semantics cannot safely be downgraded to metadata merely because its binding module is unavailable.

### R07 — The ODRL validation claim needs a narrower scope or stronger checks

These are tests of the purported ODRL layer, not complaints that due2 intentionally rejects some valid ODRL features.

| Reproduced example | ODRL requirement | Actual result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Agreement assigner and assignee are string literals | Party-valued functional roles, IM §2.1.3/§2.3/§2.6 | Both checkers accept |
| One logical node states both `odrl:and` and `odrl:or` | One operand sub-property, IM §2.5.2 | Both checkers accept; the interpreter separately declines two connectives |
| Scalar `eq` with two `rightOperandReference` values | One right reference except the specified set-valued form, IM §2.5.1 | ODRL layer accepts; due2 correctly rejects reference dereferencing in general |
| Custom Action has no `includedIn` chain to `use` or `transfer` | Action definition requirement, IM §2.4 | Both checkers accept; direct request can permit |
| Asset has `odrl:hasPolicy P`; P's permission has an action but no explicit target | Asset must be inferred as target, IM §2.2.3 | ODRL layer rejects missing target; due2 also rejects it |

The [shape](https://github.com/matthiasautrata/due2/blob/dc232b5f2fb2442c92ea3d6e5d3a39d05c106c11/conformance/odrl22-shape.ttl#L45-L133) checks action/target presence, but does not supply the full missing party-node and compact-property validation. [LogicalConstraintShape](https://github.com/matthiasautrata/due2/blob/dc232b5f2fb2442c92ea3d6e5d3a39d05c106c11/conformance/odrl22-shape.ttl#L343-L397) requires some recognized connective, not exactly one connective kind. The [reference-value property](https://github.com/matthiasautrata/due2/blob/dc232b5f2fb2442c92ea3d6e5d3a39d05c106c11/conformance/odrl22-shape.ttl#L279-L292) lacks the scalar cardinality check. The [materialized entailment set](https://github.com/matthiasautrata/due2/blob/dc232b5f2fb2442c92ea3d6e5d3a39d05c106c11/tools/check_odrl22.py#L184-L238) does not implement the mandatory inverse-target rule.

**Qualifications.** It is reasonable to require fully materialized due2 input and to leave inverse properties outside its supported fragment. It is not then reasonable to call the inverse-target example invalid ODRL. Likewise, limited SHACL validation is useful, but must not be described as encoding every relevant normative requirement until its coverage is demonstrated. The example tests should be independent of the checker being qualified.

### R08 — Several laws are not correct as written

The [law table](https://github.com/matthiasautrata/due2/blob/dc232b5f2fb2442c92ea3d6e5d3a39d05c106c11/docs/spec/due2-workflows.md?plain=1#L527-L540) is intended to be an acceptance contract. The following issues matter before translating that table into property tests:

1. **L2, structural independence.** An obligation's `in_force` depends on whether another atom of its policy matches. Holding the obligation, request, and world fixed, adding one matching permission changes `in_force` from `F` to `T`. This was reproduced directly. The weaker rule in §1—never read another atom's **finding**—can hold while the stronger L2 statement—unchanged when other atoms are added or removed—does not. State which structural policy context is fixed or precomputed.
2. **L6, duty neutrality.** A lapsed precondition closes its permission before conflict. Under `perm`, a prohibition survives before timely evidence is supplied, but is eliminated after that evidence restores the permission's candidacy. This was reproduced: the conflict elimination changes from absent to an `eliminated(..., perm)` row. Excluding only the `violated_precondition` row from L6 does not exclude this downstream change. Neutrality can be stated conditional on a fixed candidate set; the unconditional statement is false.
3. **L7, readiness.** A ready permission and no surviving `Forbid` are insufficient under `violations = deny`: a violated root obligation can still deny. This behavior is explicitly tested already. Include every denial source or restrict the law's configuration.
4. **L3, trace neutrality.** Adding an unread resolution changes the SOTW collection digest. The trace's `evaluation.sotw_id` must therefore change even if no semantic finding changes. “The trace unchanged” is too strong for a trace that includes all four input digests. State the projection that is invariant, or define an evaluated/read-only SOTW identity and use it consistently. Related replay statements need the same precision.

These examples do **not** refute the entire completion algorithm. They refute or expose ambiguous scope in the claimed acceptance laws. The mathematical arguments also need to account for the duty-driven candidate closure rather than asserting that fulfilling a duty leaves the candidate set and conflict eliminations unchanged.

### R09 — Duty instance identity and trace cardinality need another design pass

The [duty definition](https://github.com/matthiasautrata/due2/blob/dc232b5f2fb2442c92ea3d6e5d3a39d05c106c11/docs/spec/due2-workflows.md?plain=1#L96-L121) describes a binding key from authored duty plus parameter values, inherited parties, and target. The [grant key](https://github.com/matthiasautrata/due2/blob/dc232b5f2fb2442c92ea3d6e5d3a39d05c106c11/docs/spec/due2-workflows.md?plain=1#L336-L352) is explicitly just authored `source_duty` plus request reference. Yet the [instance relation](https://github.com/matthiasautrata/due2/blob/dc232b5f2fb2442c92ea3d6e5d3a39d05c106c11/docs/spec/due2-workflows.md?plain=1#L190-L205) is keyed only by instance key and has exactly one `atom_id` and status.

Consider a named, grant-scoped duty with two targets, expanded into two simultaneously required atomic duties. Under the literal grant definition, both have the same source duty and request reference, so both have the same instance key. A record performing the action on only one target can fulfil one atom but not the other. The keyed instance relation cannot represent both atom/status rows.

Member scope needs a related clarification for compound actions, alternative constraint branches, and request-subject-dependent collection bearers. If `source_duty` means the original authored UID, the expansion identity must be represented somewhere; if it instead means a stage-1 atomic duty, say so and preserve the original authored identity separately. A trace cannot rely on an undocumented interpretation of “ground binding.”

**Confidence and boundary.** This is a contract ambiguity/collision under its literal reading, not a demonstrated collision in a production key generator: that generator does not exist. [#107](https://github.com/matthiasautrata/due2/issues/107) settles byte construction, but identity semantics and one-to-many cardinality need settlement before picking those bytes. Test one compound duty, two bindings, same request; shared duties across permissions; missing references; and collection members with distinct activations.

### R10 — Later evidence can fulfil a precondition for an earlier proposed action time

The [request time](https://github.com/matthiasautrata/due2/blob/dc232b5f2fb2442c92ea3d6e5d3a39d05c106c11/docs/spec/due2-workflows.md?plain=1#L125-L134) is the instant of the proposed action and is called validity time. The [witness test](https://github.com/matthiasautrata/due2/blob/dc232b5f2fb2442c92ea3d6e5d3a39d05c106c11/docs/spec/due2-workflows.md?plain=1#L343-L359) bounds evidence by observation time and any deadline, not by request time.

Reproduced: a request dated 2026-03-01, compensation performed 2026-04-01, world observed 2026-06-01, no explicit deadline. The interpreter returns `Permit`. The witness occurred after the requested action time.

This follows the current due2 witness rule; it is not an implementation departure from that rule. The unresolved question is whether the result means “may perform the action at the specified validity instant,” “may perform it now,” or “this pending case is now eligible to proceed.” Those meanings are different. W3C [IM §2.6.5](https://www.w3.org/TR/odrl-model/#duty-perm) calls the duty a precondition.

**Disposition.** Decide the allowed relationship between request validity time, decision observation time, duty performance time, and later exercise. Either constrain the access-question domain, bound precondition witnesses by the relevant action instant, or state that re-evaluation issues a new prospective request/time. Keep historical replay separate from granting a past-dated request. Add a paired test for later-discovered **earlier** evidence versus genuinely **later-performed** evidence; these should not be conflated.

### R11–R12 — Existing units and hierarchy gaps are material qualification boundaries

[#67](https://github.com/matthiasautrata/due2/issues/67) records that units are labels only. The interpreter can compare an amount intended as USD with a EUR-labeled right operand by numeric value alone. Its explicit unit-label test confirms that behavior. The input model carries no unit with read values, so the evaluator cannot repair this by itself. Restrict unit-bearing oracle cases to an enforced same-unit contract or settle the model. These are not safe general-purpose monetary comparisons yet.

[#95](https://github.com/matthiasautrata/due2/issues/95) records that closed-world hierarchy with no module-resolution status can make failed or incomplete classification look like a definite non-match. A prohibition can disappear rather than become unknown. Completeness is therefore a load-bearing premise, not just a connector performance concern. Decide whether failed resolution creates `U`, prevents a verdict with an error, or is excluded by an explicit enforced completeness contract.

There is a related premise in completion: `unresolved` is completed as if a fitting value exists. A source failing to answer does not, by itself, prove that premise. The shell's contract must say when that assumption is warranted. The deliberate distinction between `missing` and `unresolved` is valuable, but a connector must not select the latter casually and thereby enable a definite verdict by complementary cases.

### R13 — The new evaluator port is a design boundary, not yet a complete contract

The existing issues correctly identify important blockers:

- [#108](https://github.com/matthiasautrata/due2/issues/108): no resolved operand bindings in the corpus; neither `plan` nor `decide` can interpret arbitrary constraints from the declared inputs alone.
- [#106](https://github.com/matthiasautrata/due2/issues/106): no relational `Reads` output or complete way to name required world hierarchy modules.
- [#107](https://github.com/matthiasautrata/due2/issues/107): derived-key byte construction is not portable yet.
- [#37](https://github.com/matthiasautrata/due2/issues/37), [#38](https://github.com/matthiasautrata/due2/issues/38), and [#41](https://github.com/matthiasautrata/due2/issues/41): relation-level vectors, independent brute-force W3, and a relation-level oracle face are not delivered.
- [#105](https://github.com/matthiasautrata/due2/issues/105): selection preservation needs its own executable property, including obligations of selected source policies.

R01, R02, and R09 add semantic information/cardinality questions beyond these already tracked byte/schema gaps. Complete those contracts before a new implementation invents representations that then become accidental standards.

### R14 — JSON/schema validation is structural, not semantic admission

The generated [`Instant` pattern](https://github.com/matthiasautrata/due2/blob/dc232b5f2fb2442c92ea3d6e5d3a39d05c106c11/tools/gen_schema.py#L95-L113) accepted both `2026-99-99T99:99:99+99:99` and `2026-02-30T12:00:00Z`. Its timezone-shaped syntax is not an XSD lexical/value-space validator.

A `Permit` atom with `relation = odrl:target` but no `relation_value` also passed both schema validation and [`check_invariants`](https://github.com/matthiasautrata/due2/blob/dc232b5f2fb2442c92ea3d6e5d3a39d05c106c11/tools/harness/vectors.py#L178-L223). The optional pair is not enforced. Additional referential checks, such as root/edge parent/constraint owner existence, are not comprehensive there either.

The [schema README](https://github.com/matthiasautrata/due2/blob/dc232b5f2fb2442c92ea3d6e5d3a39d05c106c11/schema/README.md) honestly delegates several invariants to other checks; that is sensible. The problem is the remaining gap between a file declared valid and an input the evaluator can meaningfully consume. Add semantic admission validation, or explicitly distinguish a merely well-shaped payload from a valid evaluator input. Generated-from-spec drift checking is useful but cannot prove that the source definitions themselves are sufficient.

### R15 — Current clingo-i is a partial, versioned witness, not the complete current oracle

The coverage figure was reproduced exactly: 400 passing scenarios and 38 unsupported. Of these, 37 need recurring-duty occurrences; one expects the `max_mixed` fallback and is classified unsupported by the harness's `exact_only` rule rather than executed by the backend.

[#103](https://github.com/matthiasautrata/due2/issues/103) explicitly records that clingo-i still implements the v0.33 listed frontier, not [decision 0026](https://github.com/matthiasautrata/due2/blob/dc232b5f2fb2442c92ea3d6e5d3a39d05c106c11/docs/decisions/0026-listed-frontier-routes-that-suffice.md). The current unit tests still assert the previous route result on a shape the authored corpus does not reach. A green corpus therefore does not establish agreement with the current normative frontier definition.

No authored case asserts `frontier_complete`; [#30](https://github.com/matthiasautrata/due2/issues/30) tracks that gap. The current `interpret` output does not expose the full normative trace, canonical collection IDs, instance keys, or all finding/basis relations. It cannot yet qualify byte-for-byte L1/L10 conformance to the proposed relational interface.

**Disposition.** Pin the oracle's semantic version and feature envelope. Implement and independently test the decided frontier, or explicitly use the previous specification for a restricted witness run. Unsupported must remain unsupported, never pass or agreement. Recurrence, fallback, full trace, multi-policy behavior, and admission failures need separate qualification rather than inference from the 400 successful scenarios.

## 6. ODRL fit: what is sound, what is chosen, and what must not be overclaimed

### Good alignment

- Permission/prohibition/duty structure, duty preconditions, functional roles, action inclusion, and compact/compound atomization are recognizable ODRL concepts.
- Explicit rejection rather than approximation of unsupported inheritance, dereferenced right operands, `implies`, and `andSequence` is a sound subset discipline.
- Shared-duty identity and role inheritance receive substantially more attention than in many practical policy systems.
- Separate ODRL validity and due2 admissibility are the right two questions, even though both implementations need the corrections above.

### due2-specific choices, not consequences of the Recommendation

The five verdicts, strong-Kleene handling, correlated completion, `Pending` route semantics, case evidence, instance scopes, duty activation, deployment violation policy, cross-policy combination, and profile-level conflict defaults belong to due2's contract. They need not be wrong because ODRL leaves them open, but a conformance claim must not present them as mandated W3C behavior.

The specification already discloses contested set-operator readings and its `isAllOf` coverage interpretation. Preserve that transparency. A `ContestedOperatorReading` warning is evidence of a declared interpretation, not proof that every author intended it. Additional list/closure vectors remain tracked in [#32](https://github.com/matthiasautrata/due2/issues/32).

### Offer versus Agreement needs a deployment-level meaning of Permit

W3C [IM §2.1.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/odrl-model/#policy-offer) says an Offer does not grant Rules. due2 evaluates offers and contracts, and the flat `policy` relation does not retain that distinction as an evaluation field. DPROD acceptance/lifecycle metadata is explicitly not evaluated.

This can be defensible for eligibility or hypothetical evaluation under offered terms. It is not, on its own, proof of an actual grant, a valid acceptance, or the assigner's authority. Before calling due2 an authorization foundation, state whether `Permit` means “satisfies these supplied policy terms” or “is authorized to exercise this action.” Policy admission/selection may establish the missing premises, but that obligation must be explicit. This review does not silently add contract-formation semantics to the evaluator.

## 7. How much confidence the tests and formal evidence justify

### Strong existing evidence

1. The hand-derived corpus has provenance and explains divergences instead of concealing them by changing an origin's answer.
2. The slow clingo encoding enumerates completions rather than copying the optimized two-extremes algorithm. That makes it a useful check on the optimization.
3. Independent Python brute-force oracles, generated draws, bounded spaces, count checks, shape-reach tests, and curated mutants test more than final verdict examples.
4. Mutation checks and generator/space shape assertions really exist. The PR suite contains mutant checks; it is incorrect to describe all mutation work as absent from CI. The largest space-exposure checks belong to the slow suite, which was also run locally.
5. Driver graph/input round trips, ordering invariance, and value-space tests are important defenses against a formally attractive but incorrectly marshaled oracle.
6. The Alloy models have already found useful issues. Their README properly says bounded absence of counterexamples is not proof.

### Limits that remain

- The direct interpreter and its differential share the specification's completion-key reading. They cannot independently establish that key's adequacy merely by agreeing. The repository already says this; [#91](https://github.com/matthiasautrata/due2/issues/91) and the formalization work are relevant.
- The value table is a trusted boundary for ASP. Existing properties are valuable, but the complete obligations and mutation demonstrations remain tracked in #91.
- The hand derivation, interpreter, abstract completion model, and Alloy models can share a semantic mistake. R03 shows why cross-source specification review matters even when several mechanisms agree.
- The Alloy resolution abstraction leaves out real instance identities, timing representation, and non-default combinations. Treating one instance as one duty is useful for folding but does not validate the missing instance/key interface.
- [Formal results](https://github.com/matthiasautrata/due2/blob/dc232b5f2fb2442c92ea3d6e5d3a39d05c106c11/formal/alloy/README.md) quantify over stated scopes; worlds constrain the number of unknowns. They are not unbounded proofs, and do not establish ODRL conformance or RDF parsing correctness.
- The prover and formal-model differential are planned/parked, not delivered. [#87](https://github.com/matthiasautrata/due2/issues/87), [#89](https://github.com/matthiasautrata/due2/issues/89), and [#86](https://github.com/matthiasautrata/due2/issues/86) make those distinctions visible. This review does **not** turn a parked prover into a mandatory prerequisite for all development; the assurance threshold is an owner decision.
- The slow suite is available but not scheduled by the current verification workflow. Reproducing it here gives evidence for this revision, not continuing regression protection.
- W1 does not exist. L9 preservation of an actual normalized corpus, the stage-1 identity/locality/bijection guarantees, and the negative normalizer contract cannot yet have passed implementation acceptance.
- Multi-policy authored cases and fact attribution are blocked by the existing case format ([#1](https://github.com/matthiasautrata/due2/issues/1)). The admission examples in this report do not close that gap.

**Interpretation of “exact.”** Current exactness is relative to the specified completion abstraction and resource fallbacks. It is not domain-complete reasoning over all real operand values. [#25](https://github.com/matthiasautrata/due2/issues/25) deliberately leaves some same-fact comparisons independent. The intended consequence is a wider `Indeterminate`, not a wrong definite verdict, under the fit/completeness premises. Document that meaning wherever an external consumer might read `exact = true` as proof of complete real-domain reasoning.

## 8. Feasibility and reasonableness

### Feasible core

The proposed in-memory evaluator over typed relations is a reasonable implementation target. Existing executable tests show that a meaningful single-policy subset can be interpreted reliably and at useful interactive speeds. The measured backend run had roughly 42 ms median and 77 ms p99 per corpus scenario on this machine; these are witness-run measurements, not a production service capacity claim.

Flat data, pure functions, explicit sources, and one-hop preassembled operands make it possible to implement the same semantics in another language. The language choice is less urgent than R01/R02/#108: another language cannot repair missing information in the contract.

### Costs that must be bounded, not argued away

- Structural cross-products and constraint DNF can grow sharply; the normalizer's exact pre-materialization limits are a good idea. Actual thresholds and diagnostics need settling with [#11](https://github.com/matthiasautrata/due2/issues/11).
- Mixed completion, frontier enumeration, and explanation construction have different costs and need separate budgets.
- A 64-variable oracle cap is not a proof of a safe worst-case runtime. Favorable measured formulas do not establish a bound for every possible formula of that size.
- Recurrence up to a finite observation instant has finitely many occurrences when it has a finite anchor, but can still generate an impractically large history. Do not call it mathematically infinite; specify an operational instance budget and failure behavior. The exact RRULE/DTSTART encoding and anchor storage also need qualification.
- Hierarchy completeness, evidence admission, provenance, and source freshness are semantic preconditions as well as operational concerns. Passing their uncertainty into the model matters more than optimizing the fold.

None of these costs makes the overall approach infeasible. They make a bounded, explicit first qualification scope preferable to a universal conformance claim.

## 9. Documentation assessment

The architecture, workflow narrative, worked examples, bibliography, and decision records provide a strong starting point for a cold reader. The technical-writing skill and generated-reference checks are useful. The report's main documentation concern is not prose polish: it is whether the prose actually fixes a portable contract.

Prioritize:

1. Resolve R01/R02/R03/R08/R09, not merely link the conflicting paragraphs together.
2. Separate current behavior, decided semantics not implemented, open design, and historically superseded decisions in every normative cross-reference. #103 is a particularly important example.
3. Reconcile absolute wording such as “every normative requirement,” “all atoms,” “nothing downstream needs more,” and “same trace” with the demonstrated exclusions.
4. Make checker coverage precise. A diagnostic listed in the registry is not evidence that its checker branch is implemented.
5. Clarify full trace conformance versus the authored-level `interpret` projection. Neither a projected source IRI nor an alpha-renamed digest is byte-level trace qualification.
6. Make replay premises concrete: engine/version provenance ([#98](https://github.com/matthiasautrata/due2/issues/98)), immutable profile/binding material in corpus identity, access to lineage, and treatment of input-digest changes under neutrality.
7. Remove the stale statement in the corpus's standing RL2 divergences that complementary permissions on an unknown fact always give `Indeterminate`; the current unresolved/complement semantics deliberately permits a definite answer by cases.

The doc-reference tests establish that references resolve. They do not establish that the referenced claims are current, mutually consistent, or semantically true.

## 11. Recommended decisions and work order

This is a recommendation, not authorization to implement changes or alter decided semantics.

### Gate A — Settle the semantic contract

1. Decide duty-expression representation and preservation (R01).
2. Complete W2/W3 timing and per-instance data (R02).
3. Resolve void-policy obligations with explicit consequences for monotonicity (R03).
4. Settle duty identity/cardinality and the two-clock access question (R09/R10).
5. Repair the law statements and test their scopes (R08).

**Exit:** a cold reader can derive the expected results of the counterexamples from one unambiguous normative contract, without reading an implementation to fill gaps.

### Gate B — Establish honest admission and ODRL coverage

1. Land near-miss negative cases for R04–R07 and implement or accurately exclude each corresponding check.
2. Reject or explicitly materialize mandatory replacement/inverse semantics at the correct boundary.
3. State the supported ODRL fragment and extension readings independently of due2's business-domain corpus.
4. Decide units and hierarchy-failure behavior, or impose an enforced restricted-oracle envelope (#67/#95).

**Exit:** known invalid input does not get a misleading “valid/admissible” classification; unsupported semantics cannot disappear into metadata.

### Gate C — Qualify a current, restricted semantic oracle

1. Bring the listed frontier into agreement with decision 0026 (#103), including the new independent definitions and full/partial frontier flags.
2. Require the report's semantic counterexamples as regression vectors.
3. Define the precise supported envelope: recurrence, unit assumptions, source completeness, policy classes, single/multiple policies, and exact-only versus fallbacks.
4. Preserve unsupported counts in the acceptance result; do not aggregate them into green coverage.
5. Rerun the slow suite and the Alloy checks after semantic changes, and document the selected assurance level. A new model run is not a proof unless it actually proves the claim.

**Exit:** the witness agrees with the chosen current semantics on every input claimed in scope, and the claim's exclusions are machine-visible.

### Gate D — Qualify the flat evaluator boundary, then normalization

1. Complete bindings, `Reads`, and stable identity contracts (#108/#106/#107 plus R09).
2. Author independent relation vectors, including full findings, deadline facts, instances, explanations, and malformed inputs (#37).
3. Build the independent brute-force W3 and relation-level oracle face (#38/#41).
4. Check W2/W3 boundary sufficiency and all stated laws, including multi-policy cases and source-policy selection.
5. Implement W1 only under the established preservation tests: `Den`/lineage for stage 1 and L9 against the corrected authored interpreter for stage 2.

**Exit:** another implementation can consume the documented wire values without guessing bindings, timing facts, grouping, or identity rules, and normalization is proven by executable preservation evidence not to change the admitted policy meaning at tested bounds.

Only after these gates should a separate decision examine witness use by the predecessor system or evaluator-foundation adoption. That later review must not weaken due2 to accommodate another implementation.

## 12. Final assessment

The design work has produced a credible semantic project with unusually explicit reasoning and meaningful verification. The local tests are not superficial: the exhaustive, differential, mutation, and driver checks are valuable evidence and were reproduced.

The central residual risk is **faithfully implementing an incomplete or internally conflicting contract**, not simply having too few unit tests. The strongest next investment is a short set of semantic decisions and independent counterexamples at the exact seams identified above. That is a narrower and more defensible task than rewriting the evaluator, adding another language, or declaring the existing green suite to be an oracle qualification.

**Recommendation: retain the architecture, repair the semantic seams, qualify a bounded current oracle, and defer foundation/adoption claims until the explicit gates pass.**
