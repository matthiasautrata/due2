## 1. Decision summary

**The remediation is substantial and mostly convincing. The architecture remains feasible and worth continuing. Yesterday's main findings must not be repeated as though nothing changed. However, closing the review issues does not yet qualify the whole specification, trace interface, or authored interpreter as an authoritative oracle.**

The strongest improvements are substantive contracts, not cosmetic edits:

- Duty disjunction has a flat representation that preserves one duty atom.
- W2 explicitly carries the fixed deadline-passed fact that W3 needs.
- Replaced policies and unrecognized profiles are rejected rather than silently interpreted.
- The four overstated laws have been qualified at the right boundaries.
- The two-clock access question and the meaning of `Permit` are explicitly decided.
- ODRL validity is distinguished more carefully from due2 admission.

Two changes are **semantic decisions**, not repairs to make behavior match yesterday's preferred interpretation. A void policy retains its root obligations, explicitly departing from literal ODRL void semantics. A `Permit` answers the supplied terms, not whether an offer was accepted or a grant exists. These are clear enough to review on their merits; they should not be described as unexplained implementation contradictions anymore.

The deeper pass finds remaining seams:

1. The keyed trace cannot represent two different unknown-input statuses for the same witness condition evaluated against two pieces of evidence.
2. The instance-key definition still does not explicitly distinguish compound actions or request-dependent performers. The two-target fix is correct but narrower than complete identity qualification.
3. The new action-hierarchy check rejects a named refined-action resource, although it accepts the corresponding anonymous representation. This also conflicts with the normalizer's skolem-only output contract.
4. Mandatory inverse-party semantics are not handled consistently between ODRL validation, admission, and the interpreter.
5. Temporal validation still differs across policy admission, relation validation, and the value table; profile-defined bearer validation and cyclic constraints expose further bounded checker gaps.

These findings do **not** demonstrate that the optimized verdict calculus is unsound on its tested, supported subset. They do demonstrate that the published port and admission envelope still need work before another implementation can safely treat them as a complete contract.

### What the repairs establish

**Duty clauses.** [Decision 0028](https://github.com/matthiasautrata/due2/blob/beb7c5fbf776250b5177610a52a82a4372985d6a/docs/decisions/0028-a-duty-condition-is-kept-in-clauses.md?plain=1) makes a coherent minimal representation choice. Root rules become alternatives; a duty retains its identity while clauses express alternative guards or performances. No guards means no guard restriction, and no witness conditions means any admissible witness suffices. An unconstrained duty is not malformed and must not be rejected merely because it has no clauses. The authored interpreter already reads trees, so its green results confirm expected meanings, not a tested clause-lowering implementation.

**Deadline information.** [Decision 0032](https://github.com/matthiasautrata/due2/blob/beb7c5fbf776250b5177610a52a82a4372985d6a/docs/decisions/0032-an-instance-records-whether-its-deadline-passed.md?plain=1) supplies exactly the fixed bit missing in yesterday's non-recurring counterexample. W3 can now distinguish false fulfilment that leaves a duty outstanding from false fulfilment that closes a route. The interpreter's internal `passed` fact was already doing this; the change makes it a public obligation.

**Time and grants.** [Decision 0030](https://github.com/matthiasautrata/due2/blob/beb7c5fbf776250b5177610a52a82a4372985d6a/docs/decisions/0030-a-re-evaluation-moves-the-request-time.md?plain=1) gives a defensible prospective access rule without contaminating the pure evaluator with orchestration. The four scenarios are individually supplied requests/worlds, not an implemented stateful case transition. [Decision 0031](https://github.com/matthiasautrata/due2/blob/beb7c5fbf776250b5177610a52a82a4372985d6a/docs/decisions/0031-a-permit-is-the-answer-of-the-terms.md?plain=1) is also clear: no acceptance flag is needed inside this evaluator to implement the chosen meaning of `Permit`. The application must establish that the selected terms are binding when it uses that result for authorization.

**Evidence keys.** A discharge names the authored-duty/request grant, while an instance additionally distinguishes the binding. This is not automatically cross-fulfilment: object, action and performer checks still choose which duty atoms a record witnesses. The correction in decision 0032 to any broader suggestion in the earlier review is accepted. The remaining question is trace identity, not evidence matching by discharge key alone.

## 5. Detailed follow-up findings

### F01 — One constraint evaluated against two witnesses has more than one input status

**Contract.** [The trace declaration](https://github.com/matthiasautrata/due2/blob/beb7c5fbf776250b5177610a52a82a4372985d6a/docs/spec/due2-workflows.md?plain=1#L192-L229) keys `unknown_part` by `atom_id`, `predicate`, `kind`, and `ref`; `input` and `status` are non-key fields. [Fulfilment](https://github.com/matthiasautrata/due2/blob/beb7c5fbf776250b5177610a52a82a4372985d6a/docs/spec/due2-workflows.md?plain=1#L345-L358) evaluates witness conditions once for each witness. Every live unknown part must be reported with the status of the input it read.

**Small reproduced example.** One compensation duty requires witness amount 5. Two otherwise admissible evidence records have the correct performer, action and object:

| Evidence | Amount input | Constraint truth | Input status |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `e1` | Absent | U | `missing` |
| `e2` | IRI where a decimal is required | U | `invalid` |

The interpreter derives `witness_status(e1,c,missing)` and `witness_status(e2,c,invalid)`, both witness values U, and fulfilment U. Its projected reply retains two unknown-status entries and returns `Indeterminate`, range `Pending` to `Permit`.

At the normative relation boundary both reads require the same key: `(d, fulfilled, constraint, c)`. They differ only in `status`. The real collection validator reports **two tuples with that key**. Picking one status discards the other read; inventing another constraint ID violates the reference to the one normalized constraint. There is no specified aggregation or precedence rule that resolves this.

**Impact.** A valid supported non-recurring duty cannot meet both the “every unknown part” requirement and the keyed trace contract. The authored-level projection avoids enforcing that key, so passing authored cases does not catch the problem. This is an explanation/input-occurrence representation failure, not evidence of a wrong verdict in the example.

**Disposition.** Decide how an evaluated part identifies its context: at least its finding and witness, with instance/occurrence considered at the same time. Alternatively define a deliberately aggregated diagnostic relation and its loss of precision. Do not silently choose a status when serializing. Pin this exact pair as a relation-level vector and require order invariance and reference resolution.

The adjacent `basis` relation also lacks a predicate or evaluation-context key. It should be checked in the same pass for per-witness truths and dual-role duties. This report's demonstrated collision is in `unknown_part`; it does not claim that every possible aggregate `basis` design is impossible.

### F02 — Fixing two targets does not finish duty-instance identity

**Contract.** [The duty binding definition](https://github.com/matthiasautrata/due2/blob/beb7c5fbf776250b5177610a52a82a4372985d6a/docs/spec/due2-workflows.md?plain=1#L117-L125) lists authored duty, parameter values, inherited party roles and target. [Instance derivation](https://github.com/matthiasautrata/due2/blob/beb7c5fbf776250b5177610a52a82a4372985d6a/docs/spec/due2-workflows.md?plain=1#L337-L343) uses that binding key directly for member scope, adding the request reference for grant scope. [Decision 0032](https://github.com/matthiasautrata/due2/blob/beb7c5fbf776250b5177610a52a82a4372985d6a/docs/decisions/0032-an-instance-records-whether-its-deadline-passed.md?plain=1#L15-L28) says the binding separates the atoms of one duty.

**Compound-action control.** One named duty has two actions, `compensate` and `inform`, with the same target and parties. One evidence record performs compensation only. Both checkers admit it. The interpreter constructs two duty atoms and derives:

- compensation: `fulfilled`;
- information: `outstanding`;
- permission verdict: `Pending`.

Those are distinct jointly required atomic duties under the stated expansion rule. But action is not expressly included in the binding-key ingredients. If an implementer follows the listed ingredients literally, both have one key and the keyed `instance` relation cannot hold their different atom/status rows. The saved relation probe confirms that such rows collide. It does **not** claim to have run a production hash generator.

**Request-dependent performer.** With a PartyCollection bearer, Alice's act fulfils Alice's request but not Bob's; the interpreter reproduces `Permit` and `Pending` respectively. With no bearer, the request subject likewise supplies the performer. A W1-time key based on a collection IRI or absent bearer is not, without another grounding rule, the person-bound identity described by the annual-training example. Distinct activations and relative deadlines make that distinction load-bearing even if verdicts are computed from evidence afresh.

**Disposition.** Before #107 fixes bytes, state all semantic identity ingredients and when they are grounded. Explicitly cover selected action, relation predicate/value, all relevant function bindings, fallback/request-subject performers, shared duties, dual roles, and occurrences. If “ground binding” already intends the whole atomic selection and runtime performer, state it and reconcile W1-time `binding_key` with that runtime dependency. This is broader than choosing a digest delimiter.

The two-target repair remains correct. This finding asks for a complete identity contract rather than reopening that repair on its original example.

### F03 — A refined-action resource is not necessarily an anonymous node

[The new shape](https://github.com/matthiasautrata/due2/blob/beb7c5fbf776250b5177610a52a82a4372985d6a/conformance/odrl22-shape.ttl#L255-L294) exempts blank nodes from the action hierarchy requirement because a refined action's `rdf:value` names its actual action. [The checker](https://github.com/matthiasautrata/due2/blob/beb7c5fbf776250b5177610a52a82a4372985d6a/tools/check_corpus.py#L876-L913) nevertheless checks every IRI explicitly typed `odrl:Action`, including an IRI used to name that same refined-action structure.

**Reproduced input.** A permission's action is `ex:act`; that resource is typed `odrl:Action`, has `rdf:value odrl:use`, and a purpose refinement. It defines no new action vocabulary term. Both the ODRL layer and admission reject `ex:act` for having no `includedIn` parent. The authored interpreter reads the refinement and returns the expected unknown-dependent result, rather than the hierarchy rejection.

W3C [IM §2.5.4](https://www.w3.org/TR/odrl-model/#constraint-action) defines the `rdf:value` representation of a refined action; it does not make anonymity a semantic requirement. The actual vocabulary action here is `odrl:use`, already a top-level action.

There is also an internal contradiction: [normalization identity](https://github.com/matthiasautrata/due2/blob/beb7c5fbf776250b5177610a52a82a4372985d6a/docs/spec/odrl-compound-to-atomic-normalization.md?plain=1#L140-L164) explicitly skolemizes refined-action nodes, while the output must contain no blank nodes and must be valid ODRL. Converting an otherwise accepted anonymous wrapper into an IRI must not turn it into a newly defined action vocabulary term that needs its own parent. W1 has not emitted such an artifact yet, so this is a contract/output-validation consequence, not an observed normalizer failure.

**Disposition.** Distinguish action vocabulary terms from resources representing a refined use of an action, by their structure and role, not merely RDF node kind. Check the action named by `rdf:value`. Add equivalent anonymous, authored-IRI and skolem-IRI vectors, plus a genuinely new action with no parent as a negative control. Do not fix the wrapper by inventing an `includedIn` edge that changes its meaning.

### F04 — Inverse parties need the same validity/admission distinction as inverse targets

W3C [IM §2.3.3](https://www.w3.org/TR/odrl-model/#policy-assigned) requires `assignerOf` and `assigneeOf` to infer the parties of every rule of the named policy. [The entailment helper](https://github.com/matthiasautrata/due2/blob/beb7c5fbf776250b5177610a52a82a4372985d6a/tools/check_odrl22.py#L232-L260) now materializes `hasPolicy` targets but not these party roles.

**Reproduced input.** An Agreement has a permission with an action and target. External party triples state `owner assignerOf P` and `alice assigneeOf P`, with no direct party properties. The ODRL layer rejects the Agreement as missing both roles, despite the mandatory inference. The due2 admission checker accepts it with no error; clingo-i declines `assigneeOf` as unsupported. Changing the policy class to Set makes the ODRL layer pass, while the admission/interpreter mismatch remains.

No incorrect `Permit` was observed from the interpreter: its decline is the safe behavior. The defects are a valid-ODRL false rejection and a misleading successful admission. An implementation built from the forward-only projection must not drop these inverse roles and widen the policy to another requester.

**Disposition.** As with decision 0034's `hasPolicy` treatment, entail the mandatory roles for structural ODRL validation and either materialize or explicitly reject them at due2 admission. State the choice, and test both inverse predicates and a request from a different assignee. This does not require dereferencing or I/O inside W2.

### F05 — Calendar checks are repaired in vectors, but policy lexical validation is still weaker

The new [vector validator](https://github.com/matthiasautrata/due2/blob/beb7c5fbf776250b5177610a52a82a4372985d6a/tools/harness/vectors.py#L47-L89) rejects yesterday's impossible calendar instants. That fix works. The policy paths still use a [timezone suffix test and a duration regex](https://github.com/matthiasautrata/due2/blob/beb7c5fbf776250b5177610a52a82a4372985d6a/tools/check_corpus.py#L247-L320), with [temporal right-operand checks](https://github.com/matthiasautrata/due2/blob/beb7c5fbf776250b5177610a52a82a4372985d6a/tools/check_corpus.py#L649-L668) that do not validate calendar values.

The saved probes reproduce:

| Input | Admission | Interpreter/value table |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Absolute deadline `2026-02-30T12:00:00Z`, typed dateTime | Accepts without errors | Declines: deadline not comparable |
| The same impossible dateTime as a typed right operand | Accepts without errors | Declines: ill-typed dateTime |
| Deadline `P1W`, typed duration | Accepts without errors | Declines: deadline not comparable; value table calls the duration ill-typed |
| SOTW instant `02026-01-01T00:00:00Z` | Relation schema and semantic validator accept | Value table calls dateTime ill-typed |

The first three also pass the ODRL shape, whose present checks are not a complete datatype validator. An RDF literal carrying a datatype IRI is not proof that its lexical form denotes a value. XSD [dateTime §3.3.7](https://www.w3.org/TR/xmlschema11-2/#dateTime) rules out February 30 and expanded years padded beyond four digits. XSD [duration §3.3.6](https://www.w3.org/TR/xmlschema11-2/#duration) has year/month/day/time components, not the ISO week form `P1W`. The checker explicitly allows `W` in its regex; the value table correctly does not.

**Impact.** A green admission run still accepts static policy terms that cannot be interpreted under the declared temporal types. The expanded-year example is a smaller newly introduced residue in the otherwise useful calendar-validator repair. These are not wrong definite verdicts in the reproduced interpreter: it safely declines the ill-typed temporal inputs.

**Disposition.** Give each boundary an explicit lexical/value-space validation obligation and cross-check policy, vector and runtime paths with near-misses. Reuse an appropriate exact validator or independently test separate implementations against the same normative cases. Keep static malformed policy terms separate from unknown/misfit runtime values. Do not assume RDFLib warning output is an admission error.

Invalid RRULE grammar is a separate, acknowledged deferral under #119, not an undisclosed failure to implement #116's reduced gate.

### F06 — Profile-defined parties escape the literal-entity check

[The DPROD module](https://github.com/matthiasautrata/due2/blob/beb7c5fbf776250b5177610a52a82a4372985d6a/profiles/dprod.ttl#L7-L10) assigns `dprod:subjectOfDuty` the bearer role. [The role specification](https://github.com/matthiasautrata/due2/blob/beb7c5fbf776250b5177610a52a82a4372985d6a/docs/spec/due2-profiles.md?plain=1#L175-L189) gives it the same semantic purpose as the default assignee. But the new literal-entity checks enumerate core predicates rather than every supported profile role.

**Reproduced input.** A compensation duty states `dprod:subjectOfDuty "Alice"`. Both checkers accept it without errors. The interpreter reads the literal as the bearer and returns `Pending`, naming a duty that an IRI-valued evidence actor cannot fulfil.

**Disposition.** Validate the resource-valued contract of the supported role predicates after profile selection, including profile-defined bearers and beneficiaries. Add a negative example beside the repaired core-assignee case. This is not a claim that arbitrary annotation literals should be rejected or that every vocabulary needs a generic inference engine.

### F07 — Constraint cycles are not a controlled admission result

**Reproduced input.** A named logical constraint refers to itself through a one-element `and` list. Calling the real admission entry point raises `RecursionError: maximum recursion depth exceeded`. The ODRL shape reports no violation; clingo-i safely returns `unsupported: constraint: cycle`.

[The recursive checker](https://github.com/matthiasautrata/due2/blob/beb7c5fbf776250b5177610a52a82a4372985d6a/tools/check_corpus.py#L514-L545) descends into connective members without a path/visited guard. The existing rule-reference acyclicity check concerns duty/remedy/consequence edges and is not a constraint-tree cycle check.

**Impact.** A tiny input causes a checker failure instead of a stable supported/unsupported or invalid-input diagnostic. This is a robustness and admission-contract gap, not evidence that cyclic constraints yield an authorization result. The report does not infer a complete ODRL cycle rule merely from this failure.

**Disposition.** State the accepted constraint graph shape, detect cycles before traversal, and pin a controlled diagnostic. Distinguish a cycle from a valid shared named subexpression, and also bound deep acyclic input rather than relying on Python's recursion limit.

## 6. Residual contract questions and known exclusions

### Recurrence remains an unfinished contract as well as an unsupported feature

The [recurrence rule](https://github.com/matthiasautrata/due2/blob/beb7c5fbf776250b5177610a52a82a4372985d6a/docs/spec/due2-workflows.md?plain=1#L359-L387) creates multiple instances per duty atom, each with occurrence-specific evidence eligibility, deadline and status. The `finding` key remains only atom/predicate. Decision 0032 says an unknown instance becomes fulfilled or not according to “its finding,” but it does not state how multiple independent occurrence fulfilments are represented.

For example, two occurrences can each have a different timely evidence record with an unknown amount. Their fulfilments can differ; one atom-level U cannot identify two independent occurrence choices. Known statuses can be carried per instance, but the occurrence-specific U dependency and its completion need an explicit representation. There is no stated rule that `fulfilled` is an aggregate OR over all occurrences; inventing that rule would make one successful occurrence stand in for another.

This review does not claim a runtime recurrence bug: clingo-i declines recurrence, #80 is open, and #119 explicitly parks grammar/anchor questions. Before admitting recurrence to an oracle envelope, settle occurrence-specific findings, witness contexts, activation anchoring, bounds and the RFC 5545 literal form together. Alternatively expressly restrict the initial relational contract to non-recurring duties. Parking implementation does not make a published contract complete.

### The relational port still needs its planned deliverables

The [schema boundary documentation](https://github.com/matthiasautrata/due2/blob/beb7c5fbf776250b5177610a52a82a4372985d6a/schema/README.md?plain=1#L37-L43) remains appropriately explicit about what it lacks:

- [#108](https://github.com/matthiasautrata/due2/issues/108): resolved operand bindings in the corpus;
- [#106](https://github.com/matthiasautrata/due2/issues/106): relational `Reads` and complete hierarchy planning;
- [#107](https://github.com/matthiasautrata/due2/issues/107): portable derived-key bytes, after semantic identity is fixed;
- [#37](https://github.com/matthiasautrata/due2/issues/37), [#38](https://github.com/matthiasautrata/due2/issues/38), [#41](https://github.com/matthiasautrata/due2/issues/41): real relation vectors, independent brute-force W3 and a relation-level interpreter face;
- [#105](https://github.com/matthiasautrata/due2/issues/105): executable preservation of request-based policy selection.

The `unknown_part` omission from the W2 signature, despite being declared in the trace and required by W3, is a smaller documentation inconsistency in this same interface pass. The schema's existence and generation-drift tests do not fill these semantic gaps.

### The current witness is not the current full frontier oracle

[#103](https://github.com/matthiasautrata/due2/issues/103) still records that clingo-i has the old listed-frontier reading. [Its implementation](https://github.com/matthiasautrata/due2/blob/beb7c5fbf776250b5177610a52a82a4372985d6a/spikes/clingo-i/lp/explain.lp#L222-L251) selects ready/pending candidates in the Kleene world rather than the routes-that-suffice construction of decision 0026. The generated abstract checker and Alloy model test the newer definition; their success does not transfer it into clingo-i.

[#30](https://github.com/matthiasautrata/due2/issues/30) still tracks corpus assertions for `frontier_complete` and its fallback. The authored reply does not expose the full canonical trace or enforce every relation key. Thus the 417 passing scenarios are useful evidence, not L1/L10 wire-level qualification.

### Units and world completeness remain semantic premises

[#67](https://github.com/matthiasautrata/due2/issues/67) is not resolved by temporal or action fixes: a monetary amount's numeric value does not identify its unit. [#95](https://github.com/matthiasautrata/due2/issues/95) concerns a failed classification lookup being read as a closed-world non-match, potentially losing a prohibition. An action having a valid vocabulary parent does not establish that a world hierarchy lookup was complete.

For a restricted witness, require comparable units and complete admitted closure modules, and reject runs that cannot establish those premises. Do not silently call failed resolution false. Domain-aware completion over other same-value comparisons remains [#25](https://github.com/matthiasautrata/due2/issues/25); `exact = true` is explicitly relative to the literal abstraction, not full value-domain reasoning.

## 7. ODRL fit after remediation

### What improved

The validity/admission split is the right architecture. An unsupported construct can be valid ODRL, and rejecting it is safer than accepting and discarding its meaning. Replacement resolution outside pure W1–W3, unrecognized-profile rejection, declared action ancestry, resource-valued parties, and connective cardinality materially improve the supported boundary.

Compound/compact expansion follows W3C [IM §2.7](https://www.w3.org/TR/odrl-model/#composition) and [§2.7.1](https://www.w3.org/TR/odrl-model/#composition-compact). due2's one-root Atomic Policy packaging and relational stage are engineering choices, not a W3C mandate to evaluate independent fragments and OR their answers. Source-policy conflict, shared duties and lineage must remain attached throughout.

ODRL/RDF at authoring/storage boundaries and typed relations for normalization output/evaluation is a sound direction. It retains semantic IRIs and provenance without requiring a general RDF engine in every component. F01–F04 concern preserving meaning across that boundary, not an argument for abandoning it.

### The important disclosed departure

W3C [IM §2.10](https://www.w3.org/TR/odrl-model/#conflict) says `invalid` voids the entire policy. [Decision 0029](https://github.com/matthiasautrata/due2/blob/beb7c5fbf776250b5177610a52a82a4372985d6a/docs/decisions/0029-a-void-policy-keeps-its-obligations.md?plain=1) deliberately keeps root obligations in force and may still deny. The revised [architecture](https://github.com/matthiasautrata/due2/blob/beb7c5fbf776250b5177610a52a82a4372985d6a/docs/design/due2-architecture.md?plain=1#L190-L197) names the departure and explains the monotonicity and anti-evasion rationale.

This is a defensible design trade-off to make explicit, but disclosure is not proof of identical ODRL semantics. A blanket “not a dialect” or “semantically sound interpretation” claim should be scoped to the admitted fragment and declared readings; the `invalid`/obligation/`deny` combination must carry its documented exception. Consumers needing literal whole-policy voiding cannot assume agreement on that combination.

The new `Permit` definition, by contrast, is a scope definition for the query: an Offer's terms can be evaluated hypothetically without pretending the Offer grants rules. The application must not confuse eligibility under supplied terms with acceptance, current lifecycle status, issuer authority, or a binding grant. Those are selection/admission/workflow responsibilities, not justification for adding an unrequested acceptance mechanism to W2.

## 8. Assurance, feasibility and documentation

### What the green checks justify

The project has meaningful assurance: authored examples, independently structured brute-force checks, generated inputs, bounded exhaustive spaces, curated mutants, parser/input checks and formal abstractions. The ordinary PR suite really includes mutation checks; only the larger exposure spaces are in the slow suite. The latter passed again here. The completion computation and bounded Alloy assertions remain good evidence at their stated levels.

The important independence limit remains: several mechanisms share the chosen completion abstraction and may share a contract omission. Neither an abstract resolution model nor an authored interpreter has to serialize `unknown_part` using the published key, so both can agree while F01 survives. Similarly, the interpreter's symbolic action-bearing atom identity can behave correctly without validating the specification's derived-key definition.

The new tests pin the examples they actually express. They do not exercise W1's real clause lowering, the real relational key generator, a stateful orchestrator moving request time, or canonical full-trace replay. These deliverables should be tested directly when written rather than credited retroactively to passing authored scenarios.

The prover remains parked by owner decision. This review does not impose a new mandatory Lean or Isabelle gate. Select the assurance threshold explicitly and preserve the distinction between bounded search, generated testing, and proof.

### Feasibility

The decomposition remains reasonable: bounded offline normalization; pure matching/performance against frozen inputs; pure candidate/conflict/frontier reduction; impure case orchestration outside them. No new framework or language is needed to address the findings. The implementation language is less urgent than specifying every identity and evaluated context the relations need.

The witness's roughly 41 ms median is encouraging for experiments, not a production capacity result. DNF expansion, mixed-variable completion, frontier enumeration and occurrence expansion have distinct budgets. A finite recurrence anchor and finite observation time imply a finite history, but not a manageable one; an explicit instance budget and failure contract are still necessary.

### Documentation

The new decision records are a strong improvement: they show alternatives, explain why behavior was retained or changed, and avoid quietly altering meaning. The updated laws and the explanation of literal-relative `exact` are notably clearer. Keep that discipline for the remaining identity/context questions.

Two smaller issues should be included in normal housekeeping:

- [The DPROD compatibility narrative](https://github.com/matthiasautrata/due2/blob/beb7c5fbf776250b5177610a52a82a4372985d6a/docs/spec/due2-profiles.md?plain=1#L244-L250) still says every example passes admission unchanged, which no longer matches the 12 expected rejections under the new hierarchy gate.
- [The ODRL conformance introduction](https://github.com/matthiasautrata/due2/blob/beb7c5fbf776250b5177610a52a82a4372985d6a/conformance/odrl22-conformance.md?plain=1#L3-L25) still contains broad certification language. Its later limitations are more precise. Passing a shape establishes its encoded checks, not all ODRL normative behavior or every datatype's validity.

These are secondary to semantic portability, not reasons for another architecture rewrite.

## 11. Final assessment

**The fixes materially improve due2. Most of yesterday's concrete defects are repaired or explicitly settled, and the substantial executable checks remain green.** That is meaningful progress and supports retaining the architecture.

The remaining critical risk is at the portable contract: what identifies a duty instance and an evaluated part, and whether the full trace can faithfully carry all supported reads. The new admission examples show why a second pass beyond the original regression set was worthwhile. None calls for another evaluator rewrite or premature integration.

**Recommendation: finish the context/identity seam, close the bounded checker gaps, qualify the current restricted witness, and then exercise the actual relational port. Do not equate closed review issues or a green authored corpus with complete oracle qualification.**
